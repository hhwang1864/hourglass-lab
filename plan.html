02 / THE IDEA

A small object. A lot to learn.

Our first playable version: a wooden frame, a clear glass vessel, 50,000 colliding grains, an orbit camera, and a satisfying flip.

01

Make it tangible

Use millimetres on the controls and metres in the physics. Show rulers, axes, a cutaway, and the grain-to-neck size ratio. Change the sand and wood colours.

02

Make it playful

Flip gravity, try lunar gravity, slow time, colour grains by speed, or shake a jam loose. Add sound and a gentle camera orbit after the sand behaves.

03

Make it measurable

Count grains crossing the neck. Watch mass stay constant. Compare frame time at 50k, 100k, 250k and 500k. Keep render quality and simulation settings visible.

Platform decision stays open. This deliverable is an HTML/CSS planning document. The simulation will not use a JavaScript/TypeScript application stack. A native GPU application is one candidate; a compiled browser target is another, subject to checking its browser integration requirements. Choose the language and platform after reviewing the mathematics. Your RTX 3060 Ti has 8 GB of VRAM; 500k interacting grains remains a measured stretch target.
03 / THE MATHEMATICS

Understand it.
Then simulate it.

Read these in order. Every equation has a job in the hourglass. Expand a topic when you want the implementation detail.

01
Coordinates & scaleHow a dimension becomes a 3D object
+

Put the neck at the origin. Use a right-handed coordinate system with +Y up; X and Z span the horizontal plane. Store positions in metres, time in seconds, mass in kilograms. Convert only at the UI boundary.

xm = xmm / 1000    ·    g = (0, −9.81, 0) m/s²

A grain is a sphere at p = (x, y, z) with radius r = d/2. A camera changes how we see it; it does not change its physical size. A mesh point moves from model space through world, view and projection matrices:

pclip = P · V · M · (x, y, z, 1)T   →   pNDC = pclip,xyz / pclip,w

M places the object, V places the viewer, and P gives perspective. If all lengths grow by s, area grows by s² and volume and mass by s³. A vessel twice as large holds eight times as much at the same packing fraction.

Try it: halve the grain diameter. Each grain now occupies ⅛ of its original volume.

02
A shape we can measureRevolve a profile, integrate its volume
+

Our planning profile has height H, maximum inner radius R, and neck radius a = D/2. For −H/2 ≤ y ≤ H/2, define b = R − a:

rwall(y) = a + b sin(2π|y| / H)
S(y, θ) = (rwall(y) cos θ, y, rwall(y) sin θ),   0 ≤ θ < 2π

This revolves a 2D curve around Y. The two end openings are closed with small disks of radius a. It is a measurable starting shape with a cusp at the neck; the final glass should use a rounded, validated profile.

Vbulb = π ∫0H/2 rwall(y)² dy
= (πH/2) [a² + 4ab/π + b²/2]

The integral adds the volumes of very thin circular slices. Expanding the square uses the mean of sin over [0, π], which is 2/π, and the mean of sin², which is 1/2. This formula gives the worked-example capacity above.

Wall thickness t is a normal offset from the interior surface, not simply an increase in horizontal radius. For a thin, smooth shell, Vglass ≈ Ainnert and mglass = ρglassVglass. Compute the actual inner/outer mesh volumes once the neck is rounded; thin-shell estimates lose accuracy at sharp curves.

03
How much sand is 50,000 grains?Count, diameter, mass and packing
+
Vgrain = πd³ / 6   ·   mgrain = ρsolidVgrain
M = Nmgrain   ·   Vbulk = NVgrain / φ   ·   ρbulk = φρsolid

N is grain count. φ is the fraction of a sand pile occupied by solid material; the rest is empty space. We assume φ = 0.60 and ρsolid = 2650 kg/m³ as editable starting values. Grain shape, friction and preparation change packing.

At the default 1 mm diameter, 50,000 spheres have approximately 69.4 g mass and 43.6 mL bulk volume. Ten times as many grains means ten times the mass and volume. The spherical model omits irregular real grain shapes.

Two different experiments: hold diameter fixed to add more sand; or hold total volume fixed and use d ∝ N−1/3 to increase resolution. The worked example uses the first mode. The future benchmark should offer both and record which was used.

For the same bulk volume at 500k, reduce a 1 mm grain to about 0.464 mm. This also changes neck-to-grain ratio and flow behaviour.

04
Signed distance fieldsWhere is the wall, and how far away is it?
+

A signed distance field φ(p) is the shortest distance to a surface, with a sign. Our convention is negative inside the empty vessel, zero on its inner wall, positive outside. Here φ denotes distance; the packing fraction in topic 03 is a different, dimensionless quantity.

φ(p) = sign(p) minq ∈ ∂Ω ‖p − q‖
Sphere example: φsphere(p) = ‖p − c‖ − R

For the hourglass, reduce 3D to the meridian point q = (√(x²+z²), y). Sample the wall profile as line segments and include the top and bottom cap segments. For each segment from A to B:

u = clamp(((q − A) · (B − A)) / ‖B − A‖², 0, 1)
qclosest = A + u(B − A)
distance = minsegments ‖q − qclosest‖

Determine the sign with an inside test against that same sampled profile. This gives the exact distance to the piecewise-linear surface of revolution, approximating the intended smooth vessel. Do not count the revolution axis as a wall. Increase samples until distance error is small relative to a grain radius.

The tempting shortcut: f(p) = √(x²+z²) − rwall(y) describes the side surface but is not a Euclidean SDF. Locally, f / √(1 + r′wall(y)²) improves the distance estimate; it is still approximate and omits caps. Do not use f directly as an exact push-out distance.
Worked collision: a grain of radius 0.5 mm whose centre has φ = −0.2 mm overlaps the wall by 0.3 mm. Move it 0.3 mm opposite the outward normal, then re-evaluate the distance.

Where the distance field is differentiable, the unit outward normal is n = ∇φ / ‖∇φ‖. Estimate each derivative with central differences, e.g. ∂φ/∂x ≈ [φ(p+εex) − φ(p−εex)]/(2ε). Choose ε relative to the geometry resolution; guard near-zero gradients and nonunique nearest points.

A grain fits if φ(p) + r ≤ 0.
If φ(p) + r > 0:   p ← p − [φ(p) + r]n

For curved walls, iterate and re-evaluate after correction. A sampled 3D texture is faster than scanning every segment per particle, but adds interpolation error and memory. Use the segment method as the correctness reference before optimising.

05
Gravity, time & contactsTurn positions into falling and piling
+

Use a fixed simulation timestep h with substeps, independent of display refresh rate. A starting experiment is 1/120 s, then split further as needed. First predict motion:

v* = v + gh   ·   p* = p + v*h

Project overlapping grains apart. For centres pᵢ, pⱼ, define C = ‖pᵢ − pⱼ‖ − (rᵢ + rⱼ), unit direction n = (pᵢ − pⱼ)/‖pᵢ − pⱼ‖, and inverse masses wᵢ = 1/mᵢ. If C < 0:

Δpᵢ = −[wᵢ/(wᵢ+wⱼ)] Cn
Δpⱼ = +[wⱼ/(wᵢ+wⱼ)] Cn
vnew = (pcorrected − pold) / h

Iterate grain contacts and wall contacts together. Handle coincident centres with a deterministic fallback direction. Use low restitution and friction so the grains settle. Friction limits tangential impulse: ‖Jt‖ ≤ μJn. A rough pile estimate is tan θ ≈ μ; real repose angles also depend on shape, rolling and solver details.

A conservative travel heuristic is ‖v‖h < r/2; it is not a guarantee against tunnelling. Fast motion and narrow necks may need swept contacts. Start with a small reference simulation and test leaking, piling and repeated flips. Position Based Dynamics paper ↗

06
When stiffness needs a meaningXPBD as a later solver upgrade
+

Extended Position Based Dynamics introduces compliance α (inverse stiffness) and an accumulated constraint multiplier λ. For constraint C:

α̃ = α/h²
Δλ = [−C − α̃λ] / [Σᵢ wᵢ‖∇ᵢC‖² + α̃]
Δpᵢ = wᵢ∇ᵢC Δλ

For unilateral contacts, clamp the updated multiplier to λ ≥ 0 and apply only the clamped change. Reset multipliers each substep initially; persistent warm-starting requires stable contact identities. With zero compliance, the position correction reduces to the rigid constraint form.

XPBD reduces stiffness dependence on timestep and iteration count. It does not eliminate errors from too few iterations, missed contacts or large steps. Contact forces can be estimated from λ/h² under this convention, useful for a later stress overlay. Keep this upgrade after the first stable pile. XPBD paper ↗

07
A GPU cannot compare every pairNeighbour search and race-free updates
+
All pairs = N(N − 1)/2
50k → 1,249,975,000 pairs   ·   500k → 124,999,750,000 pairs

Use a uniform spatial grid with cell width ℓ ≥ 2rmax. Each grain gets a cell coordinate c = floor((p − origin)/ℓ). Build sorted cell ranges or a counting/prefix-sum grid, then search the grain’s cell and its 26 neighbours.

With bounded occupancy this keeps candidate work near O(Nk), where k is nearby candidates; sorting has its own cost. Dense cells can still be expensive. Detect overflow rather than dropping contacts. Rebuild as positions change, or prove the search margin covers solver motion.

GPU threads must not update each other’s particles unsafely. Start with a Jacobi pass: read shared previous positions, accumulate each particle’s own correction, write to a separate buffer, then swap. Dense contacts may require relaxation. Keep the reference solver for comparison. NVIDIA spatial subdivision reference ↗

08
How long should it take to empty?A flow estimate, then an experiment
+

For dry, freely flowing grains through a circular outlet, use the empirical Beverloo relation as a comparison:

ṁ = CB ρbulk √g (D − kd)5/2,   D > kd
T ≈ M/ṁ

ṁ is mass flow in kg/s, D is outlet diameter, d grain diameter, and CB, k are fitted, dimensionless coefficients. Check the units: kg/m³ × √(m/s²) × m5/2 = kg/s.

Our vessel is not a calibrated silo. Wall slope, finite fill, friction and air returning through the neck affect flow. Near a few grain diameters, arches can clog the neck; the equation does not predict individual jams. If D ≤ kd, mark the estimate outside its domain rather than taking a fractional power of a negative number.

Measure instead: count downward and upward neck crossings separately over a window Δt, then compute net ṁ = (N↓ − N↑)mgrain/Δt. Fit coefficients only in continuous flow. Air coupling is deferred. Experimental Beverloo reference ↗

09
Clear glass, heavy glass, broken glassFour separate controls with different jobs
+
Property Unit Changes
Material density ρ kg/m³ Glass mass and inertia
Wall thickness t mm in UI Geometry, glass mass, optical path and strength
Absorption / refractive index m⁻¹ / unitless Tint, attenuation and bending of light
Fracture parameters Pa, Pa√m Failure under stress and flaws

For appearance, Beer–Lambert attenuation is I = I₀e−βL: β is absorption per metre and L is light’s travel distance through glass. Snell’s law is n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂. The future renderer should expose optical properties independently of material density.

Thicker walls generally reduce stress from a given internal pressure. For a thin cylindrical shell, force balance gives σhoop ≈ pR/t. Our curved hourglass under granular contacts is a different geometry; this formula is an intuition check, not a fracture predictor. Density alone does not tell us strength. MIT pressure-vessel derivation ↗

For a later educational failure model, map wall impulses to patches: ppatch ≈ ΣJnormal/(AΔt). A real crack criterion needs tensile stress and a flaw model, e.g. KI = Yσ√(πacrack) ≥ KIC. Y is a geometry factor and KIC fracture toughness. Impacts, supports and residual stress matter.

Fun version first: a clearly labelled artistic break threshold, pre-cut shards, and a sand spill. Physical fracture needs structural analysis and validation; defer it until the ordinary hourglass works.

10
What exactly are we benchmarking?Keep simulation, rendering and measurement separate
+

Plan a 32-byte record per grain: two aligned vec4 values for position/radius and velocity/inverse mass. Two full buffers cost 64N bytes: 3.2 MB at 50k and 32 MB at 500k (decimal MB). Sorting buffers, previous positions, contacts, textures and render targets add to that. This is a baseline, not total GPU memory.

Whichever platform we select, request a GPU adapter/device, inspect granted limits and handle device loss. A browser target requires a compatible WebGPU path and a secure context such as localhost or HTTPS. Browser rendering support alone does not guarantee compute support. WebGPU API reference ↗

Keep particles on the GPU between simulation and drawing. Use instancing or sphere impostors. The transparent glass adds overdraw and sorting problems; test clear, opaque and hidden-glass views separately.

60 fps → 16.67 ms/frame   ·   30 fps → 33.33 ms/frame

After a warm-up, record at least 30 seconds per preset: median and p95 frame time, physics steps, contact candidates, escaped grains and memory estimates. Record resolution, pixel ratio, solver iterations and hardware. Use GPU timestamp queries when supported; CPU submission timing is not GPU execution time. Stop increasing N if stability or frame time fails.

04 / THE BUILD PLAN

One satisfying milestone
at a time.

Start with the geometry and one falling grain. Earn the big particle count through small, visible checks.

  1. NOW · PLANNING

    Measure the world

    This HTML plan, a static concept drawing, explained formulas and a local Git folder. Agree on units, grain size, configuration meanings, language and platform.

    Done when: capacity and mass calculations agree with independent checks.
  2. 01 · GEOMETRY

    Build the wooden object

    After selecting the implementation language and GPU framework, revolve the glass profile, add an offset shell, wood caps and pillars, lighting, orbit controls and rulers. Round the neck and validate inner volume.

    Done when: a 10 mm ruler agrees with a 10 mm object; visual and collision walls coincide.
  3. 02 · PHYSICS REFERENCE

    Drop one grain, then a thousand

    Implement gravity, signed-distance walls, grain contacts and friction on a small reference scene. Try a plane, a bowl, a funnel, and then the vessel.

    Done when: grains settle, conserve count and do not escape during flips or speed tests.
  4. 03 · THE FIRST PLAYABLE VERSION

    50,000 grains on the GPU

    Port the verified steps to compute, build neighbour ranges, remove write races, and render directly from GPU buffers. Add pause, reset, flip, presets and colours.

    Done when: a full drain and repeated flips remain stable; measure performance on the RTX 3060 Ti.
  5. 04 · FIND THE LIMIT

    100k → 250k → 500k

    Profile grid construction, contacts, wall distance and drawing separately. Offer constant grain size and constant fill modes. Export reproducible benchmark results.

    Done when: each reported particle count includes performance, physics settings and stability results.
  6. 05 · PLAY

    Give the experiment personality

    Lunar gravity, slow motion, heatmaps, a satisfying flip animation and sound. Then optional cracking and shards with an explicitly artistic failure rule.

    Done when: the extra effects preserve the stable simulation and can be toggled off for benchmarks.
Our first coding session: build the measured 3D wooden frame and glass, with a single falling sphere and a collision debug view. The 50k solver comes after this reference behaves correctly.
